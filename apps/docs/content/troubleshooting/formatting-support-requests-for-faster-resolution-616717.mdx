When you contact Supabase Support, a clear and focused ticket helps our team understand the issue, assess its impact, and begin investigating.

If you use an LLM to help write your ticket, give it the information you have gathered, such as your notes, exact error messages, and relevant evidence, combined with the prompt below. The LLM will organise this information into a concise, technically useful support ticket while removing repetition, unsupported assumptions, and irrelevant details.

Before submitting the generated ticket, review it carefully to confirm that all details are accurate and that it does not contain passwords, API keys, access tokens, personal data, or other sensitive information. An LLM should help organise the information you provide, not invent missing details.

## Prompt

Prioritise clarity over completeness. Include only information that helps the support team understand, reproduce, or investigate the issue.

Remove repetition, generic explanations, irrelevant background, and unsupported diagnoses. Distinguish observed behaviour from suspected causes, and do not present assumptions as established facts.

A shorter ticket containing verified facts is preferable to a longer ticket containing assumptions, but do not omit important technical details just to reduce the length.

**Use the following structure where relevant:**

**Summary:** One or two sentences describing the specific problem.

**Impact:** What is blocked or degraded, who is affected, and whether the issue is ongoing.

**Expected behaviour:** What should happen.

**Actual behaviour:** What happens instead, including the exact error message where available.

**Steps to reproduce:** The shortest sequence of steps supported by the information provided. If the issue cannot be reproduced consistently, say so. Do not invent steps or claim to have reproduced the issue.

**Environment:** Only relevant identifiers and configuration, such as project reference, region, product or feature, client or library version, and approximate timestamps with timezone.

**Troubleshooting completed:** Briefly list actions already attempted and their observed results.

**Evidence:** Include relevant logs, queries, screenshots, or request IDs. Use focused excerpts instead of full log dumps, preserving exact error messages, code, queries, timestamps, and identifiers.

**Request:** State clearly what assistance or outcome is needed from Support.

**Requirements:**

- Aim to keep the ticket under 300 words, excluding essential logs or code. Treat this as a guideline, not a hard limit. Include additional detail when it materially helps Support investigate the issue.
- Put the most important information first.
- Do not repeat information across sections.
- Do not invent, assume, or infer missing details. Use only the information provided.
- Clearly distinguish observed facts from hypotheses. Label relevant suspected causes as unconfirmed.
- Adapt the structure to the issue. Omit irrelevant or empty sections rather than adding generic filler.
- Preserve exact error messages, code, queries, timestamps, and identifiers. Do not silently alter or paraphrase technical evidence.
- If essential information is missing, draft the ticket using the available facts and list no more than three specific questions under **Missing information**. Include only questions whose answers would materially help Support investigate the issue.
- Check the output for passwords, API keys, access tokens, credentials embedded in connection strings, and unnecessary personal or sensitive information. Replace sensitive values with descriptive placeholders such as `[REDACTED_API_KEY]`, while preserving the surrounding technical context.
- Before returning the ticket, verify that factual claims are supported by the provided information, uncertainty is clearly labelled, and sensitive information has been redacted.
- Do not include greetings, apologies, conclusions, or commentary about how the ticket was written.
