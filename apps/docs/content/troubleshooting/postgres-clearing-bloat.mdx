A plain vacuum marks dead space as reusable for future writes, but it does not compact the table.

```sql
-- dead rows will be cleared, but they will leave behind a lot of free space
vacuum my_table;
```

To immediately return free space to the system, the table must be rewritten. `vacuum full` does this, but takes the table offline until it completes or fails:

```sql
vacuum full my_table;
```

Instead of `vacuum full`, consider the [`pg_repack` extension](/docs/guides/database/extensions/pg_repack). It achieves the same result without taking the table offline.

<Admonition type="caution">

Both `vacuum full` and `pg_repack` create a full copy of the table, potentially doubling the disk space the table claims for the duration of the operation. Check your [I/O and storage charts](/dashboard/project/_/observability/database) beforehand. If your system is constrained, consider modifying your [disk settings](/dashboard/project/_/settings/infrastructure) before cleanup.

</Admonition>

### Manage vacuum settings

Review the [Autovacuum management](/docs/guides/database/postgres/autovacuum-management) docs to tune the vacuum for your workload. This can minimize bloat accumulation in the future.
