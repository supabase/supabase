Rather than yet another name tag destined for the trash, every [Supabase Select](https://select.supabase.com/) attendee received a programmable badge, that they can continue to hack long after the conference.

During the conference it was an animated name tag with the schedule and a couple of games on it. Since then, people have turned it into a live flight tracker, an arcade game about dead tuples, and a language model that runs entirely on the badge.

Here’s how we went about building the badges, and some ideas to push it even further.

## Why a badge

I'd seen electronic badges at conferences like GitHub Universe and DEF CON and wanted Supabase to have one, partly because I love electronics and mostly because I think microcontrollers are an underused way for developers to get more out of the skills they already have.

If you can build a web or mobile app, you already know most of what it takes to put a screen on your desk, or a sensor in your room that does something useful when a reading changes. The hardware is inexpensive, and was not affected by the recent price increase of memory - Raspberry Pi raised the price of their computers several times, while microcontrollers remained the same price. With a tool like Claude Code, a first hardware project becomes an afternoon spent mostly on deciding what to make.

The brief was to give people something that works as a name tag for two days and stays useful long after.

## What's inside

The badge is based on the [Pimoroni Tufty 2350](https://shop.pimoroni.com/products/tufty-2350) and ships with:

- An RP2350 microcontroller
- A color screen
- A rechargeable battery
- Onboard Wi-Fi and Bluetooth
- Physical buttons (A, B, and C) you can map to anything

## Making a thousand of them

Our first iteration was a board with a Raspberry Pi Pico, a screen, and a battery, attached so you could pull them apart and use them as a starter kit once the conference was over. It was a great solution for building a modular conference badge, but assembling a thousand of them by hand was not feasible.

<Img
  alt="The prototype badge: a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W, a screen, and a battery holder on one board"
  src="/images/blog/how-we-made-the-supabase-select-2026-badge/prototype.jpg"
/>

With our next iteration, we went with [Pimoroni](https://shop.pimoroni.com), who build similar hardware - screen, battery and programmable microcontroller - but on a single board, greatly reducing the complexity of assembly. We wrote a custom OS and all the software required: the name tag, the schedule, and the games. It was only a fraction of what the badge was capable of, but it was exactly what we needed _at_ Select.

## Hack your badge

Everything you need is at [badge.select](https://badge.select/):

- If you have a physical badge, the [first app guide](https://badge.select/guides/first-program) walks you through adding your own app over USB.
- If you want a coding agent to help, the [badge project kit](https://badge.select/guides/code-with-an-agent) gives Claude Code the API reference and instructions it needs. You can try something like: "Read the project instructions and badge API reference. Build a pomodoro timer with a large countdown. Use A to start or pause, B to reset, and C to switch between work and break sessions."
- If you weren't at Select, the [virtual badge](https://badge.select/virtual) and the [Make editor](https://badge.select/make) let you build in your browser.
- If you want to go further, replace the firmware and work in C or Rust. There's already [a guide](https://badge.select/guides/apple-ii) for turning the badge into an Apple IIe emulator, with a physical USB keyboard and BASIC programs running on the badge itself.

Good first projects are small and useful: a clock with exactly the display you want, a pomodoro timer beside your keyboard, a display that reads from your Supabase project over Wi-Fi, or a light that turns on when your coding agent needs you. With Supabase Realtime, the badge can react the moment something changes in your app. Your first change can be as small as making one button do something different.
